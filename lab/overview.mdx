<p className="al-lede">A place to put iterations side by side, decide what's actually best, and keep a record of why. Everything on this site was worked out here first.</p>

## Why it exists

My favorite and most used feature of Figma was alt-dragging a frame to duplicate it, then making one adjustment. I'd build rows and rows of versions, each slightly different, compare them side by side, ask teammates for feedback and comment right on them.

Most vibecoding workflows lose that. You see one output, it generally looks "good", and there's little incentive to iterate on it or to make a real human decision about what to keep and what to change. That's why so many vibecoded sites end up looking the same: once people see something, they don't change it.

## The idea

I think design sense comes from two things: being able to come up with different options, and deciding which one brings delight, serves the project, or gives the best experience. More chances to make those decisions means more chances to sharpen that sense.

So alongside the actual site, the project gets a lab:

- **Options, side by side.** The coding agent generates several versions of a screen or feature, each as its own frame, so I can compare them like rows of Figma frames.
- **Toggles to mix and match.** Backgrounds, fonts and colors can be switched across every option at once, to see how a choice holds up in different layouts.
- **A record of decisions.** Each round of what I tried, kept and dropped goes in the process log, so earlier versions stay visible instead of being implemented and reverted.
- **Only then, the real site.** When something wins, it gets built into the site, and the design system documents what shipped.

The result is less pressure on any single attempt, more deliberate choices, and a clear trail from the first prototype to the final site.
